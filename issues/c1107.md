Offering a first draft of the missing vocabulary, in case it is useful. Happy to open it as a PR against whatever path you would serve it from, or for you to take it and change anything, no attribution needed.

It declares the thirteen terms the exporters actually emit, drawn from the emitting call sites in 0.6.5 rather than from what a vocabulary "should" contain, so nothing here is invented for completeness:

- **Classes**: `sem:Entity` (the fallback type in `serialize_to_turtle`), `sem:Relationship` (the reified form in the JSON-LD export)
- **Entity terms**: `sem:text`, `sem:confidence`, `sem:metadata`
- **Relationship terms**: `sem:related_to`, `sem:source`, `sem:target`, `sem:type`
- **Document terms**: `sem:entities`, `sem:relationships`, `sem:exportedAt`
- **Temporal**: `sem:openEndedInterval`, domain `time:Interval`, range `xsd:boolean`
- **Provenance**: `sem:role_generator`, typed `prov:Role` so `prov:hadRole` points at a declared value

Three judgement calls worth flagging, since they are the parts most likely to be wrong:

1. `sem:confidence` and `sem:metadata` carry no `rdfs:domain`, because the exporters emit them on both entities and relationships. Constraining them to `sem:Entity` would make the relationship exports non-conformant.
2. `sem:exportedAt` is ranged `xsd:dateTime` rather than `xsd:dateTimeStamp`, because `datetime.now().isoformat()` produces no timezone offset and `dateTimeStamp` requires one.
3. `sem:text` overlaps `rdfs:label` in intent. I declared it rather than aliasing it, since aliasing is a modelling decision that belongs to you.

The draft is here: [semantica-ns.ttl](https://github.com/fabio-rovai/open-ontologies) (73 triples, parses under a strict RDF 1.1 parser).

For what it is worth on the effect: with no vocabulary, a closed-world check of a Semantica export cannot run at all and reports that nothing was checked. With this file loaded, the same export comes back clean, thirteen declared terms, four predicates and one type checked, nothing undeclared. That is the difference between "no tool can tell whether these terms are real" and "these terms are real, here is the document that says so".
